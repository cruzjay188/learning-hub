    The short version

    Problem
    v1 tracked my work but didn't move me forward. Today pointed at a lecture, not a step. On my phone, rating and notes sat 8,000px down the page. Lectures I had attended showed up in amber as "Not started".
    What I did
    Ran a heuristic review with 8 task walkthroughs and 38 screenshots, wrote a brief from 14 Mobbin searches, rebuilt the hub, re-audited it, fixed what was left, then extracted the design system and a motion system.
    Result
    Every severity-3 problem from v1 is fixed or mostly fixed. Today ends in one button that finishes the current step. Controls pass contrast in both themes, and there's a ⌘K palette and keyboard shortcuts.
    Not proven yet
    Every score comes from my own review of a tool built for one user: me. The next step is a task test with five classmates, set out at the end.

    Context

    One user, a packed day, and a phone between classes

    Code Platoon runs AI and full-stack engineering for 15 weeks, Monday to Friday. Each day has Stand Up, a lecture, work time, a team meeting and Stand Down. Every lecture comes with homework, and the course moves on whether you finished or not. The user is me, so I could test every idea against a real day.

    What do I do right now?

    At 11:15 between activities, on a phone. Needs a step, not a page to read.

    Did I actually finish that?

    Ticking off homework steps, honestly, without the app nagging about days I was in class.

    What's slipping?

    Lectures I felt shaky on should come back for review before they're gone.

    Constraints

    • One static HTML file. No build step, no framework, vanilla JS.
    • Runs in a sandboxed page: nothing loads except fonts from Google.
    • Light and dark are both first-class. Works from 390px wide.
    • Progress saves to the page's database, with a browser fallback.
    • Keep the Code Platoon teal, and give orange exactly one job.
    • Course data is generated from the calendar. The UI can't invent dates.

    Decision log

    Three calls that shaped v2

    Each one gave something up. These are the trade-offs I'd defend in a review.

    Decision 1 · Today view

    Up next names a step and can finish it

    Context

    v1's card said "Today's lecture · Open guide". To do anything I had to open the guide, scroll, and find step 1.

    Options

    Keep the lecture card and add a progress ring

    Show the step itself, with "Done when" and a button that ticks it

    Chose

    The step. Uxcel's "Continue learning" card names the current lesson and the time left. I borrowed that and took it one level down, to the step, because a step is what fits between classes.

    Cost

    Today now needs per-step data and an Undo toast, and the card is taller. You can try it in the mockup at the top of this page.

    Decision 2 · Color semantics

    Honest, not guilty

    Context

    On day one, nine lectures I'd attended showed as amber "Not started", because the hub didn't exist when I did the work.

    Options

    Streak pressure: amber or red for anything overdue

    Neutral grey for "not ticked", amber only for real problems, orange only for now

    Chose

    Neutral. Each color gets one job: teal for progress, orange for now, amber for a real problem.

    Cost

    Less urgency. I accepted that because guilt makes people stop opening an app, and this one only works if I open it daily.

    Decision 3 · Layout

    A ledger, not a dashboard

    Context

    v1's rail had three meters reading 0%, 0/10 and 15%. They didn't change during a day and didn't lead to any action.

    Options

    Summary tiles with big numbers

    Dense rows on hairlines, with progress inside each row: rings, mini-bars, counts

    Chose

    Rows, like Linear and GitHub. Progress sits where you act on it. Panels are rare, so the one that remains (Up next) stands out.

    Cost

    Less "wow" in a screenshot. The page looks quieter than a typical dashboard, and that was the point.

    Motion system

    Motion explains what changed

    Every motion answers "what just happened?": a tick landed, the next step arrived, the week goal was met. Nothing moves for decoration. UI motion stays under 300ms, and things you do a hundred times a day, like ⌘K or keyboard shortcuts, don't animate at all.

    How I worked

    AI agents as a review team

    I used Claude Code with two specialist agents I configured: a UX auditor and a UI designer. They did a lot of the work. This section says which parts.

    What the agents produced

    • Both heuristic reports, with screenshots and measurements
    • The Mobbin research and the v2 design brief
    • Code drafts for v2, the system generator and the motion QA script

    What I owned

    • The question the product answers, and the bar: "would an experienced product designer ship this?"
    • The constraints, the review loop and its order
    • Which findings to fix and what shipped

    How it was checked

    • Every claim tied to a screenshot or a measured number
    • A second audit against the same 8 tasks, not a fresh, easier list
    • Frame captures mid-animation, and a reduced-motion run

    Why say this up front? The speed (two audits, a system and motion in two days) only makes sense with the AI. What I want you to judge is the direction and the decisions, and those were mine.

    Reflection

    What I'd do differently, and what's next

    Differently

    • Start on the phone. Every one of v1's worst problems was on the phone, where I actually use it between classes.
    • Write the tokens before v2, not after. Pulling the system out of shipped code kept it honest, but naming colors earlier would have prevented the orange overload in v1.
    • Get a second user sooner. I wrote both the tasks and the answers.

    Next

    • Task test with 5 classmates. The same 8 tasks, timed, think-aloud. The key measure is time to find "what do I do now".
    • Watch the review loop for a full cycle. Ratings come back after 1 to 10 days, so it needs two weeks of real use.
    • Make sync freshness visible on the page, the one finding still only partly fixed.
